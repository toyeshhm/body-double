# Demo video script (2:45)

Meta's rubric, and where the video earns each part:

| Criterion | Weight | Shown at |
|---|---|---|
| Human Connection | 30% | 1:30 map, 2:05 quests, 2:20 ending line |
| AI Centrality | 25% | 0:40 mirror, 1:10 the Wolf adapting, 1:45 notebook, 2:32 curve |
| Originality | 20% | 0:00 hook, 1:45 notebook, 2:05 quests |
| Execution & Demo | 25% | the whole video runs on the live site with real phones |

## Before recording

- Record the host screen at **1920x1080** in a full-screen browser, so the Wolf curve shows on the recap.
- Use **3 to 5 real phones** (friends nearby), or 2 phones plus **Demo mode** (adds Hazel, Rowan and Juniper as bot players).
- Set **3 to 4 chapters** in the rules to keep the take short. The adaptive targeting line needs chapter 3 or later.
- Play one full practice game first. It warms up the database and gives the Wolf curve data.
- Capture one phone screen too (screen recording on the phone), for the cutaways marked **PHONE**.
- One full game uses roughly 30 to 50 AI calls (more players, more calls). Two takes is fine; avoid a dozen.

## Shot list

**0:00 to 0:12: hook** (host screen, landing page)
> "Spot-the-AI games already exist. They end at a scoreboard. Body Double ends with a plan."

**0:12 to 0:30: join** (host lobby with QR code, then PHONE)
- Friends scan the QR code, pick a name and a storybook token, and agree to the rules.
> "One screen, everyone on their phone. You agree to let an AI Wolf borrow your voice for one night."

**0:30 to 0:40: warmup** (PHONE)
- Answer a warmup question. Show the "Say it" mic button once.
> "First, the Wolf studies how each of you writes: your slang, your punctuation, your emoji."

**0:40 to 1:10: a chapter** (host screen)
- Question appears. Answers come in. Cut to the magic mirror with A and B.
- PHONE: vote.
- Reveal: the mirror shows the real answer and how many villagers the Wolf fooled.
> "Each chapter, the Wolf writes a fake in one friend's voice. Everyone else has to pick which one their friend really wrote."

**1:10 to 1:30: the Wolf adapts** (host screen)
- Tells: a player types what gave it away ("too many commas").
- Next chapter's intro: "The Wolf noticed nobody really knows ___ yet."
> "When you catch it, you tell it why. It remembers. By chapter three it goes after whoever this group knows least."

**1:30 to 1:45: the forest map** (host)
- Lines between people who know each other's voice.
> "Every vote also says who knows whom. Here's the village's map."

**1:45 to 2:05: the notebook** (host, right after the map)
- The Wolf's first fake beside its last: "It knew only the warmup answers" vs "It knew every earlier answer and their style", and the fooled counts under the headline.
- Its notes on each villager, and the tell that caught it.
> "Then the Wolf opens its notebook: its first fake next to its last, and what it learned about each of you. It got better."

**2:05 to 2:20: quests** (host)
- A pair plan with its quoted evidence, then the group plan and poster.
> "So it ends the night by planning real hangouts for the pairs who barely know each other, built on things they actually wrote."

**2:20 to 2:32: the tale and the privacy promise** (host recap, then "The End")
> "A story of the night, a picture to save, and then the book closes and every answer is deleted."

**2:32 to 2:45: how it's built** (recap's Wolf curve, then one slide or the README's AI table)
> "Every vote is logged anonymously on Tiger Data, so across every game tonight you can watch the Wolf's fooled rate climb by chapter." (Read the real numbers off the curve.)
> "Muse writes the Wolf and plans the quests. Gemini moderates and learns each player's style. Tiger Data tracks whether the Wolf is getting smarter. It all runs on Vercel."
> "Body Double. Come for the game, leave with a plan."

## If something goes wrong on camera

- A fake takes more than a few seconds: keep talking over the "The Wolf is brewing ___'s voice" screen. The game moves on by itself after 25 seconds with a backup fake.
- A phone drops: it rejoins from the same link and keeps its score.
- Quests or the recap look generic: that's the backup text, which means an AI call timed out. Re-record that segment rather than the whole video.
